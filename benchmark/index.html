VariantEvidenceBench

The task: given a variant that was uncertain at a fixed point in time, produce a calibrated probability that it is pathogenic, or decline. The grade comes only from what happened next.

Test protocol. Training data is ClinVar frozen in January 2024, with 1,103,657 labelled rows. The test set is the 27,748 variants that were uncertain at that freeze and were later reclassified: 8,856 pathogenic and 18,892 benign. The labels are the later reclassifications. Labels are evaluation targets only, never features and never evidence.

Abstention and safeguards

Reported separately from accuracy, and never averaged into AUROC, ECE or Brier. A system that scores everything can look complete while inventing the missing evidence. This benchmark counts the declines as a result in their own right.

The cite-or-decline bar

The live model gives a probability only where the evidence for that variant meets a minimum bar of 3 evidence items. Below that bar it declines. On this test set it declined 17,391 of 27,748 variants (62.7%). A decline is the correct output where the evidence is thin, and it is published here at the same size as the accuracy numbers.

A failed lookup is never evidence

Where a lookup fails or is impossible for a variant form, that variant receives no probability and declines. A failed or impossible lookup is never dressed up as evidence, and an unobserved record is only used where the lookup itself was actually retrieved.

Fabricated citations count as failure

Every claim in an evidence report must cite a record that supports it. An unsupported or fabricated citation counts as a failure, not as a near miss and not as partial credit.

Kept separate, on purpose

There is no combined score on this page that mixes accuracy with abstention. AUROC, ECE and Brier describe the variants a configuration scored. The decline counts above describe the variants it refused. Both are needed to judge a system, and neither is hidden inside the other.

Methodology and provenance

Data sources

ClinVar, frozen January 2024 release, for the training labels and for the set of variants uncertain at the freeze. AlphaMissense precomputed missense scores. REVEL scores, from the public release archived as Zenodo record 7072866. gnomAD allele frequencies and VEP most severe consequence, retrieved through Ensembl VEP. One provenance note carried from the run record: Ensembl serves its own gnomAD freeze, so the allele frequencies are Ensembl's served freeze, which is not necessarily the current gnomAD release.

Temporal truth rule

Models are fitted on the frozen January 2024 data only. They are graded on classifications that appeared after that freeze. A refreshed current ClinVar release was not used as a feature or as evidence for any row, because the current classification is the test label, and using it would be label leakage.

Fitting and calibration

Isotonic calibration is fitted only on train holdouts, never on the test set. For the candidate tranches, each tranche uses its own train population, its own gene prior, and isotonic calibration fitted on that tranche's own train holdout. A calibrated probability is mandatory for a variant to be scored at all, and the minimum evidence bar of 3 items applies to every row.

Honest limits

  • Labels are partly predictor-influenced. Post-cutoff reclassification labels are themselves partly influenced by predictor scores. This inflates the apparent performance of all rows, the live row included, and it is stated here rather than corrected away.
  • Small tranches are not interpretable. The AM-only test cell in the candidate run has n of 16. Its metrics are reported in the run record for completeness and are not interpretable at that size, so no row for it appears in the leaderboard. The T2 row, at n 666, is labelled adopt with caution for the same reason.
  • T3 sees less signal. Tranche T3 discrimination is inherently weaker than the missense predictor tranches, because allele frequency, consequence and a gene prior cannot see missense-specific signal. Its numbers are published as they are.
  • Coverage and quality trade off. The live model scores 37.3% of the test set. The candidate stack reaches 99.5% coverage on a differently composed set. Higher coverage is not, by itself, a better model.
